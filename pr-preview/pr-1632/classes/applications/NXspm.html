3.3.2.37. NXspm¶

Status:

application definition, extends NXsensor_scan

Description:

Scanning Probe Microscopy (SPM) is a branch of microscopy that utilizes a physic …

Scanning Probe Microscopy (SPM) is a branch of microscopy that utilizes a physical probe to scan the surface of sample and image it at the atomic level.

The application class NXspm is designed as a skeleton and contains common technical concepts for specific SPM sub-techniques such as STM, STS, AFM etc. In addition, it can be utilized to describe the SPM experiments without further specialization for each sub-technique.

Frames of reference

SPM has no beam, so the default NeXus (McStas) coordinate system does not apply. NXspm uses five right-handed Cartesian frames. The scanner frame is the central frame: every other frame is defined relative to it, directly or through one intermediate frame.

  • Scanner frame (central): origin at the undeflected piezo position (zero offset, zero scan angle, Z piezo at its zero) or at the centre determined by the scanner calibration. x and y are the lateral drive axes of the scanner; z is normal to the sample plane and points from the sample towards the probe. It describes the relative tip-sample displacement, independent of whether the tip or the sample is moved.

  • Scan frame (one per scan): origin at the centre of the scan area, x along the fast scan axis, y along the slow scan axis. It is placed in the scanner frame by the scan offset and the scan angle.

  • Probe frame (AFM): origin at the clamped end of the cantilever on the chip, x along the cantilever long axis from the chip towards the tip, z normal to the cantilever. It is oriented in the scanner frame by the cantilever tilt and its in-plane angle.

  • Stage frame: the sample stage or coarse positioner, including the sample holder. It is placed in the scanner frame by the stage position. If no stage position is recorded, it coincides with the scanner frame.

  • Sample frame: origin at a fiducial on the sample, z normal to the sample surface. It is placed in the stage frame by the mounting translation and rotation.

The relationships follow the depends_on direction of NXtransformations, which points towards the scanner frame:

scan frame   --[offset, scan angle]---------+
probe frame  --[tilt, in-plane angle]-------+--> scanner frame
stage frame  --[stage position]-------------+
sample frame --[mount translation, rotation]--> stage frame

Conventions: rotations about z are counter-clockwise positive when viewed from +z (looking down from the probe). The measured data stay on the grid of the scan frame and are not resampled; only the transformations are recorded.

The scanner frame, the central frame of reference of SPM (see the frames of reference in the description of NXspm). It replaces the default NeXus (McStas) coordinate system.

The scanner frame is fixed by the instrument and its scanner calibration. It changes only on recalibration or when the scanner is exchanged. All lateral and vertical quantities of the piezo scanner (e.g. scan offsets, scan angle, piezo ranges) are expressed in this frame.

Every transformation chain in this entry ends here. As this is the only NXcoordinate_system in the entry, a depends_on that is not given refers to this frame.

  • calibrated piezo position: The centre of the scanner frame as determined by the scanner calibration, which also determines the directions of the axes. It can differ from the undeflected piezo position.

The sample stage or coarse positioner (e.g. stick-slip piezo motors, stepper motors), including the sample holder that moves with it. It defines the stage frame: the coarse position of the sample relative to the tip, with axes nominally parallel to the axes of the scanner frame. The stage frame is fixed between coarse moves. The stage positions are given as the transformations of this group. If no stage position is recorded (e.g. Nanonis files), the stage frame coincides with the scanner frame.

transformations: (required) NXtransformations ⤆

The stage position as a transformation chain that ends in the scanner fr …

The stage position as a transformation chain that ends in the scanner frame:

stage_z -> stage_y -> stage_x -> scanner_frame

Each axis may move either the sample or the head (e.g. on a tip-scanning instrument the XY stage may move the sample and the Z motor the head). The vector of each axis therefore gives the direction in which the sample moves relative to the tip for a positive value. A misalignment between the stage axes and the scanner axes is assumed to be zero.
